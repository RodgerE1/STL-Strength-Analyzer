How the recommendations work

For pulling, the chosen axis is the pull direction. For beam bending, it is the beam's length: bending creates longitudinal tensile stress on one side. The ranking favors keeping that direction within the layer planes. Compression, torsion and unspecified loads use printability only, because their critical stresses need more information.

The settings are starting presets: 3 / 4 / 6 walls and 20–55% infill depending on profile and compression. Estimated extrusion width is 1.125 × nozzle diameter; actual widths and wall overlap come from your slicer. A 0.4 mm nozzle starts at 0.20 mm layers (0.16 for demanding use); a 0.6 mm nozzle starts at 0.28 mm. Smaller layers are chosen for detail and consistency, not claimed to guarantee higher strength.

Thickness is the nearest opposing surface along an inward face-normal ray at sampled face centers. It is approximate and may miss a small notch or an angled feature. The edge check does not detect self-intersections or prove that a model is a valid solid. Thickness, volume and section areas are withheld for open, inconsistent, or multiple-shell meshes. Existing inward winding is normalized only for queries; export preserves the imported winding.

Faces near the build plate are used to estimate flat contact. Curved contact and slicer first-layer effects are not modeled. The ranking is a heuristic tradeoff, not an optimizer over every possible rotation. Build-volume fit also allows a 90° rotation on the bed; leave room for a brim and supports.

Inspect the exported model and toolpaths in PrusaSlicer or OrcaSlicer. Check thin-wall paths and reinforce loaded roots, webs and fastener regions with extra geometry or local modifiers. Test the finished part under the intended load.
